Risks and proposed edits

Each risk is tied to your tool, followed by what to change and the guideline behind it.

Bias and fairness

Cost or utilization used as a stand-in for health need

High priority
The risk
Your model is trained on a stand-in measure and uses past costs, zip code, and insurance type, so it scores people who got less care in the past as lower need. See case 1 in section 4.
Proposed edits
  • Write down why each target and input is a valid proxy.
  • Compare scores with a direct clinical measure.
  • Report score and selection rates by race, sex, age, language, and payer.
  • Re-check at each retrain.
Guidelines
Recommended by the CHAI Responsible AI Guide (2024), Fairness Stage 3 and NIST AI RMF MEASURE 2.11. Related law: 45 CFR 92.210.

Performance reported only overall, not by subgroup

High priority
The risk
Overall accuracy can hide higher error rates for some groups, which here means wrongly denied or delayed coverage.
Proposed edits
  • Report error rates by race, sex, age, language, disability, and payer before go-live.
  • Set unacceptable gaps in advance.
  • Repeat after launch.
Guidelines
Recommended by FDA/Health Canada/MHRA Good Machine Learning Practice, Principle 8.

Human oversight

Tool acts without a person able to stop it

High priority
The risk
Automatic coverage actions reach members before anyone reviews them. See cases 2 and 3 in section 4.
Proposed edits
  • Require review by a qualified person before any adverse or irreversible action.
  • Build an override and a stop switch with a named owner.
  • Log every automated action.
Guidelines
Recommended by NIST AI RMF 1.0, MAP 3.5 and MANAGE 2.4.

No appeal or override path

High priority
The risk
Members harmed by an automated determination can't challenge it.
Proposed edits
  • Publish a human-review route.
  • Let clinicians override without penalty and record the reasons.
  • Include appeal outcomes in monitoring.
Guidelines
Recommended by NIST AI RMF 1.0, MEASURE 3.3 and MANAGE 4.1.

Coverage decisions lean on the model instead of the individual

High priority
The risk
Relying on a score alone can overlook patients with complex needs, who are least able to appeal.
Proposed edits
  • Base each determination on the individual's record.
  • Track denial, overturn, and delay rates by subgroup.
Guidelines
Recommended by the CHAI Responsible AI Guide (2024), Stage 6.

Companion-style chat and transparency

Users form emotional dependence on the chatbot

High priority
The risk
A chat that remembers and sustains conversation can entangle lonely or vulnerable members.
Proposed edits
  • Limit relationship-style behavior.
  • Say it is AI at the start and periodically.
  • Flag heavy or late-night use and point to human support.
Guidelines
Recommended by NIST AI 600-1, section 2.7. Related law: SB 243.

Members not told AI is involved

High priority
The risk
Members who don't know AI is involved can't ask questions or opt out.
Proposed edits
  • Disclose AI use in plain language in every channel.
  • Offer a human alternative.
Guidelines
Recommended by Joint Commission/CHAI guidance (2025), Element 2.

Privacy and security

Recording without notice or consent

High priority
The risk
Recording calls without all-party consent breaks California's rule.
Proposed edits
  • Announce recording and get consent before it starts.
  • Make withdrawal easy.
  • Log consent and set retention limits.
Guidelines
Recommended by Joint Commission/CHAI guidance (2025), Element 2. Related law: Penal Code 632.

Patient data leaks through a vendor's systems

High priority
The risk
Substance use disorder records could flow to the vendor behind your base model.
Proposed edits
  • Sign business associate agreements limiting use, retention, and training.
  • Strip identifiers before sending data.
  • Verify where data is processed.
Guidelines
Recommended by Joint Commission/CHAI guidance (2025), Element 3.

Real-world cases

Documented failures that share a feature with your tool. Each is linked to a risk above.

CaseWhat happenedWhat it means for your toolEvidence
1. Care-management risk algorithmOptum · 2019 · stand-in target

A widely used algorithm for picking patients for extra care predicted future cost. At the same risk score, Black patients were considerably sicker than White patients; the authors estimated that correcting this would raise the share of Black patients flagged from 17.7% to 46.5%.

Optum reran the analysis and worked with the researchers on non-cost measures.

 Your model is trained on a stand-in measure and uses past costs. Check what the target actually measures before launch. EstablishedPeer-reviewed study
2. Medicaid budget formulaIdaho · 2016 · no basis, no appeal

A class of about 4,000 people with developmental disabilities challenged Medicaid cuts set by the state's automated formulas, which the state called trade secrets. The court found the cuts arbitrary, held they violated due process, and ordered testing of the automated systems.

 Automatic coverage actions need a stated basis and a real way to contest them. EstablishedCourt ruling
3. Post-acute care denialsUnitedHealthcare · 2023 · coverage by model

A pending class action alleges UnitedHealthcare used the nH Predict tool to deny Medicare Advantage members' post-acute care regardless of treating physicians' recommendations. The court let contract claims proceed in 2025; the allegations have not been decided.

 If model output feeds coverage decisions, keep an individual clinical review and an appeal path. AllegedPending lawsuit
4. Website chatbotOutside health · Air Canada · 2024

The airline's chatbot told a customer he could claim a bereavement fare after travel, contrary to policy. A tribunal rejected the argument that the chatbot was responsible for its own statements and held the airline liable.

 You answer for what your member-facing chat says. Test it against your actual policies. EstablishedTribunal decision

Established: found by a peer-reviewed study, regulator, court, or the organization itself. Alleged: claimed in a lawsuit or complaint that has not been decided. Settled: resolved without a finding.
